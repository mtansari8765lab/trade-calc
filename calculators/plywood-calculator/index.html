About this plywood calculator

Plywood math is area divided by sheet size: sheets = ceiling(area × (1 + waste) ÷ sheet area), where a 4 × 8 ft sheet is 32 sq ft. A 20 × 12 ft subfloor is 240 sq ft, and with 10% waste that is 264 sq ft ÷ 32 = 8.25, rounded up to 9 sheets. Our plywood guide works through the same count with sheet sizes, layout waste, and the OSB question.

The waste allowance is the whole game. A plain rectangle laid in straight rows wastes little — the last sheet in each row is cut, and the offcut usually starts the next row, so 10% is honest. But an L-shaped room, a floor with many openings, or a diagonal layout wastes far more: every cut leaves an offcut too short or too narrow to reuse, and a 45-degree layout can push waste toward 15% or beyond. Stagger the joints — end joints in adjacent rows should land at least 16 inches apart (one joist bay) — and never line up four corners at one point, which is the classic subfloor squeak.

Plywood and OSB are both sheet goods, and this calculator counts either: the sheet sizes and the math are identical. The difference is the job, not the count — OSB is the usual subfloor and roof sheathing (cheaper, and its strands interlock well), while plywood is the usual choice where the sheet will be seen, bent, or exposed to repeated wetting (it holds screws better at edges and dries out without swelling as much). Thickness is a separate decision: 3/4-in (23/32-in) for subfloors on 16-in joist spacing, 1/2-in for roof sheathing, 1/4-in for underlayment — the sheet count does not change with thickness.

How to use

  1. Measure the length and width in feet (split L-shaped areas into rectangles and add them).
  2. Choose the sheet size your supplier stocks — 4 × 8 ft is most common.
  3. Pick a waste allowance — 10% for a plain rectangle, 15% for many cuts or a diagonal layout.
  4. Press Calculate for the sheet count.

Good to know

  • Rectangular areas only, or rectangles added together. Round, curved, and heavily notched areas need the area measured in sections.
  • This counts sheets, not thickness or grade. Thickness (1/4-in, 1/2-in, 3/4-in) and grade (CDX, AC, underlayment) are separate choices that do not change the count.
  • Offcuts are assumed partly reusable at 10% waste. A diagonal layout or a room with many small cut-outs can waste more — use 15% or measure row by row.

Formula & standards

Formula: Sheets = ceiling(area × (1 + waste) ÷ sheet area); a 4×8 ft sheet = 32 sq ft.

Standard: None — sheet sizes are manufacturer standards (4×8 ft most common); no code governs sheet counts.

Waste allowance: Cutting waste at edges and around openings; offcuts rarely fit the next row, and diagonal layouts waste more.

Frequently asked questions

How many sheets of plywood do I need for a 20x12 floor?

A 20 × 12 ft floor is 240 sq ft. With 10% waste that is 264 sq ft ÷ 32 = 8.25, rounded up to 9 sheets of 4 × 8 ft plywood.

How many square feet does a sheet of plywood cover?

A 4 × 8 ft sheet covers 32 sq ft; a 4 × 10 ft sheet covers 40 sq ft; a 4 × 12 ft sheet covers 48 sq ft.

What waste percentage should I add for plywood?

10% for a plain rectangle laid in straight rows; 15% for L-shaped rooms, many openings, or a diagonal layout where offcuts cannot be reused.

Is OSB the same as plywood for counting sheets?

Yes — the sheet sizes and the math are identical. OSB and plywood differ in use (subfloor vs visible work), not in the count.

How thick should subfloor plywood be?

3/4-in (23/32-in) is standard for subfloors on 16-in joist spacing; 1/2-in suits roof sheathing; 1/4-in is underlayment. Thickness does not change the sheet count.
