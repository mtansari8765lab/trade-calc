About this epoxy garage floor calculator

Epoxy quantity is floor area × coats × (1 + waste), divided by coverage per gallon, rounded up — and the coats multiplier is the part people miss. A 20 × 20 ft two-car garage is 400 sq ft, but two coats need 800 sq ft of coverage before waste, not 400. Coverage per gallon is product-specific and thickness-specific: 100%-solids epoxy at a pro-grade 10 mils covers about 160 sq ft per gallon, while a water-based DIY product at thin build covers roughly 260 — always use the coverage printed on your product, not a generic figure. Our epoxy guide covers systems, prep, and the kit math.

Two coats is the standard build: the first coat seals the slab, the second carries the wear layer. One coat wears quickly under cars; three coats suit heavy builds and metallic floors. Rough, porous, or heavily stained concrete drinks more on the first coat — use the 15% waste figure there, and patch cracks and prime bare concrete first so the epoxy does not soak in unevenly.

Flake is the decorative broadcast thrown into the wet base coat: a light accent uses about 2 lb per 100 sq ft, a full broadcast (where the base coat disappears entirely) about 5 lb per 100 sq ft. A 400 sq ft garage at full broadcast needs about 20 lb of flake. Kits bundle this differently — a typical 500 sq ft kit covers a 250 sq ft floor twice — so if your product is sold as a kit, enter the kit's per-coat coverage in the coverage field and read the result as kits.

How to use

  1. Measure your garage length and width in feet, wall to wall at the widest points.
  2. Choose coats — 2 is standard — and enter your product's coverage per gallon from the label.
  3. Pick a waste allowance and your flake level (none, light accent, or full broadcast).
  4. Optionally enter the price per gallon, then press Calculate.

Good to know

  • Coverage per gallon varies by product and target thickness — the label figure governs; this calculator cannot know your product's solids content or mil build.
  • Does not include primer, crack filler, topcoat, or prep materials (grinder rental, degreaser, acid etch) — budget those separately.
  • Moisture matters: concrete that fails a moisture test (ASTM F1869/F2170/D4263) needs a vapor-barrier primer first, or the coating can delaminate.

Formula & standards

Formula: Gallons = ceil(floor area × coats × (1 + waste) ÷ coverage per gallon); decorative flake = area ÷ 100 × lb per 100 sq ft.

Standard: None — coverage and surface-prep guidance come from the product label.

Waste allowance: Roller and brush loss plus uneven floor absorption.

Frequently asked questions

How much epoxy do I need for a 2-car garage?

A 20 × 20 ft (400 sq ft) garage at 2 coats and 200 sq ft per gallon needs 800 sq ft of coverage — 5 gallons with a 10% waste allowance. A 500 sq ft kit covers the same garage twice over two kits.

How many square feet does a gallon of epoxy cover?

It depends on solids and thickness: 100%-solids epoxy at 10 mils covers about 160 sq ft per gallon; water-based DIY products at thin build cover roughly 260. Use your product's label figure.

How many coats of epoxy should a garage floor get?

Two coats is standard — a sealing base coat plus a wear coat. One coat wears quickly under cars; three coats suit heavy builds and metallic floors.

How much flake do I need for a garage floor?

A light accent uses about 2 lb per 100 sq ft; a full broadcast uses about 5 lb per 100 sq ft — about 20 lb for a 400 sq ft garage at full broadcast.

Can I use this calculator for epoxy kits instead of gallons?

Yes — enter the kit's coverage per coat in the coverage field and read the gallon figure as kits. A 500 sq ft kit coats a 250 sq ft floor twice.
