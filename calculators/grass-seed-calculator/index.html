About this grass seed calculator

Grass seed math is area times rate: pounds = area ÷ 1,000 × rate (lb per 1,000 sq ft), with overseeding at about half the new-lawn rate. A 2,000 sq ft new lawn in tall fescue at 6 lb per 1,000 sq ft needs 12 lb of seed; overseeding the same lawn needs about 6 lb. Our grass seed guide covers the rates by grass type, the new-vs-overseed difference, and the prep that makes seed take.

The rate is set by the grass, not the lawn. Kentucky bluegrass seed is tiny, so 3 lb per 1,000 sq ft covers it; tall fescue seed is large, so it takes about 6 lb; perennial ryegrass takes about 8 lb; fine fescue about 5 lb; bermuda about 2 lb (hulled). Those are typical new-lawn rates — the rate printed on the bag you buy governs, because blends differ and some bags are mostly filler or coating. A bag that is 50% coating by weight needs twice the pounds to put down the same seed, so read the pure-live-seed percentage, not just the bag weight.

This is the seed alternative to sod: our sod calculator covers instant lawn by the roll, and seed is the cheaper, slower route — roughly a tenth of the cost of sod, with 2–3 weeks to germination and a full season to fill in. Seed fails most often from the surface, not the rate: seed that dries out once after germinating dies, so the first two weeks need light watering two or three times a day, and seed sown on unprepared, compacted soil washes away or never contacts the ground. Rake the top inch loose, spread evenly (half the seed in one direction, half at right angles), and press it in with a roller or your feet.

How to use

  1. Measure the lawn length and width in feet (split irregular lawns into rectangles and add them).
  2. Choose the grass type — the rate is set by the seed size of that grass.
  3. Choose the job — a new lawn at the full rate, or overseeding at about half the rate.
  4. Press Calculate for the pounds of seed.

Good to know

  • Rectangular lawns only, or rectangles added together. Irregular lawns need the area measured in sections, minus beds, paths, and the house footprint.
  • Rates are typical new-lawn figures per grass type. Blends, coated seed, and regional varieties differ — the rate printed on your seed bag governs.
  • This counts seed only. It does not count starter fertilizer, straw or peat cover, or the water a new lawn needs in its first two weeks.

Formula & standards

Formula: Pounds = area ÷ 1,000 × rate (lb per 1,000 sq ft); overseeding uses roughly half the new-lawn rate.

Standard: None — rates are typical per-grass-type figures; seed-bag labels and extension-service guidance govern, and the rate printed on your bag wins.

Waste allowance: Overlap on passes and thin spots; buy the bag size above your pounds, not below.

Frequently asked questions

How much grass seed do I need for 2,000 square feet?

A 2,000 sq ft new lawn in tall fescue at 6 lb per 1,000 sq ft needs 12 lb of seed. Overseeding the same lawn needs about 6 lb.

How much grass seed per 1,000 square feet?

It depends on the grass: tall fescue about 6 lb, perennial ryegrass about 8 lb, Kentucky bluegrass about 3 lb, fine fescue about 5 lb, bermuda about 2 lb per 1,000 sq ft for a new lawn.

How much grass seed do I need for overseeding?

About half the new-lawn rate — for example, about 3 lb per 1,000 sq ft for tall fescue instead of 6 lb. Overseeding thickens an existing lawn rather than starting one.

Is grass seed cheaper than sod?

Yes — seed costs roughly a tenth of sod for the same area, but it takes 2–3 weeks to germinate and a full season to fill in, while sod is an instant lawn.

Why did my grass seed not grow?

The usual causes are seed drying out after germinating (new seed needs light watering two or three times a day for two weeks), seed sown on compacted soil with poor seed-to-soil contact, or seed buried too deep.
