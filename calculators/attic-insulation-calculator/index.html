About this attic insulation calculator

Insulation is rated by R-value — resistance to heat flow — and attic targets run R-30 to R-49 depending on climate (R-38 is the common middle target; cold climates push toward R-49). This calculator takes your attic's area, subtracts what you already have, and converts the gap into inches to add for your chosen product: blown fiberglass at R-2.5 per inch, blown cellulose at R-3.7, or fiberglass batts at R-3.2.

To find your current R-value, measure the depth of existing insulation and multiply by its R-per-inch — or read it off the product if you know it. Old, compressed insulation insulates worse than its label depth suggests; if yours is flattened or patchy, treat the current value as the lower end of your estimate.

Bag counts for blown insulation are the softest number here, because manufacturers' coverage per bag changes with target depth. This calculator assumes about 25 cubic feet of blown material per bag and says so on the result — always confirm against the coverage chart printed on the bag you actually buy, since that chart is the authority.

How to use

  1. Measure your attic length and width in feet (the insulated floor area).
  2. Pick your current R-value — measure existing depth × R-per-inch if you are unsure, or 0 for no insulation.
  3. Pick your target R-value (R-38 is a common target; R-49 for cold climates).
  4. Choose your product, then press Calculate for inches to add and quantity.

Good to know

  • Bag estimate assumes ~25 cu ft per bag of blown material — real coverage varies by brand and target depth; the product's coverage chart governs.
  • Assumes a flat attic floor; vaulted ceilings, dormers, and kneewalls need separate measurements.
  • This is a quantity tool, not an energy audit. Ventilation (soffit/ridge balance), vapor control, and local code all matter — and air sealing comes first.

Frequently asked questions

How much insulation do I need for my attic?

Most US homes target R-38 to R-49 in the attic. A 1,000 sq ft attic going from R-11 to R-38 with blown cellulose needs about 7.3 inches added — roughly 25 bags at typical coverage. Enter your numbers above for your attic.

How do I know my current attic R-value?

Measure the depth of existing insulation in inches and multiply by its R-per-inch: fiberglass batts ~3.2, blown fiberglass ~2.5, cellulose ~3.7. Four inches of old fiberglass batts is roughly R-13.

Is blown-in or batt insulation better for attics?

Blown insulation covers irregular joist bays and gaps better and is usually faster for attics; batts are easier to DIY in open, regular bays and can be layered over existing insulation. Both work when installed to the full target depth.

Should I remove old attic insulation first?

Usually no — clean, dry existing insulation can stay and be topped up, as long as it is not wet, moldy, or contaminated. Remove it only if it is damaged or blocking air-sealing work.

Do I need to air-seal before insulating?

Yes. Insulation slows heat flow but does not stop air flow; leaks around wiring, plumbing, light fixtures, and chimney chases bypass insulation entirely. Seal first, then insulate.
