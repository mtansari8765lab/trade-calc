About this stair calculator

Stair math is one division and one rule: divide your total rise by a target riser height, round up to whole risers, then divide again for the actual riser height. This calculator does that, then lays out the treads, total run, and stringer length — and flags anything outside common residential code limits (7 3/4 in max riser, 10 in min tread, handrails at 4+ risers).

A comfortable stair lands around a 7–7.5 in riser with a 10–11 in tread, giving a 32–37° angle. The classic rule of thumb: riser + tread should total roughly 17–18 in. Steeper than that feels like a ladder; shallower eats floor space for no comfort gain.

The stringer length is the diagonal from top to bottom — rise² + run², square-rooted. Buy stringer stock longer than the number (a 2×12 for cut stringers), and remember cut stringers need at least 5 in of solid wood below the notches. Always confirm with your local building department — codes amend these numbers.

How to use

  1. Measure the total rise in inches — floor to floor, or deck surface straight down to the ground.
  2. Enter your target riser height (7.5 in is a comfortable default) and tread depth (10 in minimum).
  3. Press Calculate for riser count, actual riser height, treads, total run, and stringer length.
  4. Check the code notes, then confirm with your local building department before cutting.

Good to know

  • Straight runs only — L-shaped, U-shaped, and winder stairs need landing and turn math this tool does not do.
  • Code checks reflect common US residential (IRC) limits; local amendments vary — verify before building.
  • This is a layout tool, not structural or code advice. Stringer spacing, attachment, and guards must follow local code.

Formula & standards

Formula: Risers = ceil(total rise ÷ target riser); actual riser = rise ÷ risers; treads = risers − 1; total run = treads × tread depth; stringer = √(rise² + run²).

Standard: Residential stair limits per IRC R311.7 (max 7¾-in riser, min 10-in tread, handrail 34–38 in) — the calculator flags layouts outside those limits. Confirm with your local building department.

Waste allowance: No waste field on this calculator; it is layout math, not a buy list.

Frequently asked questions

How many steps do I need for a 4 foot high deck?

A 48 in rise divided by a 7.5 in target riser gives 7 risers (6.86 in each) and 6 treads. More generally: risers = total rise ÷ target riser, rounded up; treads = risers − 1.

What is the standard stair riser height?

Residential code caps risers at 7 3/4 in; 7–7.5 in is the comfortable range. All risers in one flight must be uniform within 3/8 in.

What is the standard tread depth?

10 in minimum measured nosing to nosing in residential code; 10–11 in is comfortable. Deeper treads suit exterior stairs.

How do I calculate stringer length?

Square the total rise, square the total run, add them, and take the square root — that diagonal is your stringer length. Buy stock longer than the result.

Do my stairs need a handrail?

In most residential codes, any flight with 4 or more risers needs a handrail at 34–38 in above the nosing line, on at least one side.
