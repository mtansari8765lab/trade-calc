About this board foot calculator

Lumberyards sell hardwood by the board foot, not by the board. One board foot is 144 cubic inches of wood — the volume of a board 12 inches wide, 12 inches long, and 1 inch thick. The formula is (thickness in inches × width in inches × length in feet) ÷ 12.

Note that lumber dimensions here are actual dimensions, not nominal. A "2x4" is actually 1.5 × 3.5 inches — but board-foot pricing for construction softwood is usually quoted per piece anyway. This calculator shines for hardwoods (oak, walnut, maple, cherry) where per-board-foot pricing is the norm and a miscalculation gets expensive fast.

Enter an optional price per board foot and the calculator totals your materials cost — handy when comparing species or deciding whether that walnut upgrade fits the budget.

How to use

  1. Measure the actual thickness and width of your boards in inches and the length in feet.
  2. Enter how many identical boards you need.
  3. Optionally add the price per board foot from your lumberyard quote.
  4. Press Calculate for total board feet and estimated cost.

Good to know

  • Uses actual (not nominal) dimensions — measure the wood, don't trust the label.
  • Does not account for waste, defects, or planing loss; buy 15–20% extra for furniture-grade work.
  • Prices vary wildly by species, grade, and region — treat the cost figure as a planning estimate.

Frequently asked questions

What is a board foot?

A board foot is a unit of lumber volume equal to 144 cubic inches — for example, a board 1 inch thick, 12 inches wide, and 12 inches long. Hardwood lumber is priced per board foot.

How do you calculate board feet?

Multiply thickness (inches) × width (inches) × length (feet), then divide by 12. A 1×6 that is 8 feet long = (1 × 6 × 8) ÷ 12 = 4 board feet.

Do I use nominal or actual dimensions?

Actual dimensions. A nominal 2x4 is really 1.5 × 3.5 inches, which changes the board-foot count. Always measure the real wood.

How many board feet are in a 2x4x8?

Using actual dimensions (1.5 × 3.5 in, 8 ft): (1.5 × 3.5 × 8) ÷ 12 = 3.5 board feet.

Why is hardwood sold by board foot instead of by the piece?

Because hardwood comes in random widths and lengths from the mill. Board feet normalize everything to pure volume, so a wide short board and a narrow long board are priced fairly.
