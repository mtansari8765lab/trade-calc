About this asphalt calculator

Asphalt tonnage is one formula: area × thickness × 145 lb per cubic foot, divided by 2,000. The 145 lb/ft³ figure is the standard in-place density for compacted hot-mix asphalt (the Asphalt Institute range is 142–148). Thickness is the input that swings the answer most: doubling thickness from 2 to 4 inches doubles the tons, which is why the coverage per ton — about 83 sq ft at 2 inches, 55 at 3, 41 at 4 — is worth checking before you order. Our asphalt guide covers thickness choices, base prep, and hot-mix vs cold-mix.

Three inches of compacted asphalt over a 4–8 inch crushed-stone base is the standard residential starting point; 2 inches works only as an overlay on sound existing pavement or for very light use, and heavy vehicles or freeze-thaw climates push toward 4 inches. What you enter here is compacted depth — the loose layer the paver lays down is thicker before the roller gets to it, so do not inflate the number for compaction; the waste allowance covers edges and compaction losses.

For small jobs, compare bagged cold-mix against a plant order: cold-mix is only for patches and temporary repairs (it lasts 1–3 years), while a plant's hot-mix minimums and delivery fees can make a tiny driveway disproportionately expensive per ton. Get two or three delivered prices before committing — regional material prices run roughly $90–$200 per ton in 2026.

How to use

  1. Measure your driveway length and width in feet (for L-shapes, split into rectangles and add the areas).
  2. Enter the compacted asphalt thickness in inches — 3 inches is the standard residential depth.
  3. Pick a waste and compaction allowance (5% is standard) and optionally a price per ton.
  4. Press Calculate for cubic yards, tons, coverage, and cost.

Good to know

  • Rectangular areas only — irregular driveways should be split into rectangles and added.
  • Density is fixed at 145 lb per cubic foot; if your supplier quotes a different density, scale the tonnage proportionally.
  • Material estimate only — base stone, edging, labor, delivery, and sealcoating are separate costs. Installed driveways typically run $3–$12 per sq ft depending on region and scope.

Formula & standards

Formula: Area × compacted thickness ÷ 27 = cubic yards; tons = cubic feet × 145 lb/cu ft ÷ 2,000.

Standard: None — 145 lb/cu ft is the standard compacted-asphalt density figure used across the industry; no code governs the arithmetic.

Waste allowance: Covers uneven subgrade and edge trimming. Thickness here is compacted depth, not loose depth.

Frequently asked questions

How much asphalt do I need for a 50x10 driveway?

A 50 × 10 ft driveway at 3 inches thick is 125 cubic feet = 9.06 tons before waste — order about 9.52 tons with a 5% allowance.

How many square feet does one ton of asphalt cover?

About 83 sq ft at 2 inches thick, 55 sq ft at 3 inches, and 41 sq ft at 4 inches, at 145 lb per cubic foot.

How thick should an asphalt driveway be?

At least 3 inches of compacted asphalt over a 4–8 inch crushed-stone base for residential use. Two inches suits overlays only; heavy vehicles and cold climates need 4 inches or more.

How much does a ton of asphalt cost?

Roughly $90–$200 per ton for material in 2026 depending on region (about $120 national average for hot mix), before delivery and installation. Small orders cost more per ton.

Hot mix vs cold mix asphalt — which should I use?

Hot mix for anything permanent: new driveways, parking areas, and roads. Cold mix is only for temporary patches and pothole repairs.
