About this sod calculator

Sod is sold by the roll (10 sq ft each, typically 2×5 ft) and by the pallet (about 450 sq ft, roughly 45 rolls). This calculator converts your lawn's square footage into both, with a waste allowance for cutting around edges, curves, and obstacles — because a lawn that is 1,500 sq ft on paper always needs more than 150 rolls in practice.

The waste allowance is the part most people get wrong. A simple rectangle needs 5–10%; lawns with curved beds, trees, and sidewalks need 15% or more. Running short mid-installation is expensive — a second delivery or a patch job weeks later never matches. Order the extra; leftover rolls patch thin spots.

Confirm roll size and pallet coverage with your supplier before ordering — some farms sell slabs around 2.7 sq ft instead of 10 sq ft rolls, which changes the piece count (but not the total area). And prep the soil before the truck arrives: sod roots into loose, amended soil, not hardpan.

Worked example: sodding a 50×30 lawn

A 50×30 ft lawn is 1,500 square feet. Add 10% waste for cutting around beds, curves, and edges: 1,500 × 1.1 = 1,650 sq ft. Sod rolls cover about 10 sq ft each: 1,650 ÷ 10 = 165 rolls. Pallets hold roughly 450 sq ft: 1,650 ÷ 450 = 3.67, rounded up to 4 pallets.

Four pallets is a full delivery truck for most suppliers — which is convenient, because sod must be laid the day it arrives. Pallets heat up fast in the sun and sod starts dying within 24–48 hours on the pallet, faster in summer heat. Never schedule delivery before the soil prep is done.

Buying tips

Prep the soil before the truck arrives, not after: rough-grade, remove debris, spread 2–3 inches of topsoil or compost, rake level, and roll out a starter fertilizer. Grading after delivery wastes the sod’s short freshness window, and sod laid on lumpy ground gives you a lumpy lawn forever.

Order 5–10% more than the raw area even on simple rectangles — every curve, tree ring, and sidewalk edge turns full rolls into cut waste. And confirm your supplier’s roll and pallet sizes when you order: most rolls are ~10 sq ft and pallets ~450 sq ft, but both vary, and the delivery ticket is what you actually pay for.

How to use

  1. Measure your lawn length and width in feet. For irregular lawns, break the area into rectangles and add them.
  2. Choose a waste allowance — 10% for simple shapes, 15–20% for curves and obstacles.
  3. Optionally enter the price per roll for a cost estimate, then press Calculate.
  4. Confirm roll size and pallet coverage with your supplier before you order.

Good to know

  • Assumes standard 10 sq ft rolls and ~450 sq ft pallets — supplier sizes vary.
  • Covers sod quantity only — not soil prep, topsoil, delivery, or installation labor.
  • Irregular lawns are approximated as rectangles; add extra waste for complex shapes.
  • Order sod for delivery the morning you lay it — pallets heat up fast and sod starts dying within 24–48 hours on the pallet, faster in summer.
  • Finish soil prep before the truck arrives: grade, topsoil, level, and starter fertilizer all go down first. Sod laid on lumpy ground stays lumpy.

Formula & standards

Formula: Rolls = ceil(lawn area × (1 + waste) ÷ roll coverage); pallets = ceil(lawn area × (1 + waste) ÷ pallet coverage).

Standard: None — roll and pallet coverage come from the supplier.

Waste allowance: Cuts around beds, paths, and curves.

Frequently asked questions

How much sod do I need for 1000 sq ft?

1,000 sq ft needs 100 standard 10 sq ft rolls before waste — order 105–110 with a 5–10% cutting allowance, which is about 2–3 pallets.

How big is a roll of sod?

The most common roll is 2×5 ft (10 sq ft) and weighs 30–45 lbs. Some suppliers sell smaller slabs — always confirm before calculating.

How many rolls of sod are on a pallet?

About 45 rolls per standard pallet, covering roughly 450 sq ft. Pallet coverage varies 400–500 sq ft by supplier.

How much does sod cost?

Sod material typically runs $0.30–$0.80 per sq ft depending on grass type and region; installed with labor it roughly doubles. A 1,000 sq ft lawn often costs $600–1,000 for materials alone.

Can sod wait a day before installation?

Only in cool, shady conditions — and even then it stresses. Install the day it arrives, water deeply, and keep it moist for the first two weeks.

How many square feet are on a pallet of sod?

About 450 square feet per pallet (suppliers vary from 400–500), in rolls of roughly 10 sq ft each. Our 50×30 ft example needs 1,650 sq ft with waste = 165 rolls = 4 pallets. Confirm your supplier’s sizes when ordering.

When is the best time to lay sod?

Early fall is ideal — warm soil for rooting, cool air, and less watering than summer. Spring works well too. Avoid peak summer heat: new sod in 95°F weather needs watering two or three times daily just to survive.

How much should I water new sod?

Keep it consistently moist (not soggy) for the first two weeks — usually daily watering, twice daily in heat. Once the roots grab (tug-test a corner at 2–3 weeks), taper to deep watering two or three times per week.

Is sod better than grass seed?

Sod gives you a finished lawn in a day but costs roughly 3–5× more than seed. Seed is cheaper and offers more grass varieties, but needs 6–8 weeks of careful watering and weed control before it is usable. Choose sod for instant results, seed for budget and variety.
