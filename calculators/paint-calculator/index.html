About this paint calculator

Running out of paint mid-wall is how you end up with a visible lap line that haunts you for years. The fix is simple arithmetic: wall area × coats ÷ coverage per gallon, rounded up. Paint cans print their coverage — typically 350–400 sq ft per gallon on smooth, primed drywall — and this calculator uses your number or the 350 default.

Two coats is the honest default for most repaints. One coat works only when refreshing the same color with quality paint; three coats (or a primer coat plus two) is the reality when going from dark to light. Primer gets its own calculation if you are painting bare drywall or making a big color jump — run the numbers once for primer, once for topcoat.

Buy all your paint at once, and from the same batch if possible. Tint varies slightly between mixes, and a wall finished from two different gallons can show a faint line in raking light. One spare gallon for future touch-ups is cheap insurance.

How to use

  1. Measure each wall (length × height) and add them up; subtract large windows/doors if you want a tighter number.
  2. Enter the total square feet and choose your coats.
  3. Adjust coverage per gallon from the can if it differs from 350.
  4. Press Calculate — buy the rounded-up gallon count, all at once.

Good to know

  • Textured, brick, or unprimed porous surfaces drink far more paint — add 20–30%.
  • Ceilings need flat ceiling paint; the math is the same but buy a separate product.
  • Sprayers waste more paint to overspray than rollers; pros add ~15% for spray application.

Frequently asked questions

How much paint do I need for a 12x12 room?

Four 12×8 walls = 384 sq ft. Two coats at 350 sq ft/gal = 2.2 gallons → buy 3 gallons. Subtract ~20 sq ft per door and ~15 per window if you want it tighter.

How many square feet does a gallon of paint cover?

About 350–400 sq ft per coat on smooth primed walls. Porous or textured surfaces drop that to 250–300.

Is one coat of paint enough?

Only for refreshing the same color with good paint. Color changes and bare drywall need primer plus two coats for an even finish.

How do you calculate paint for a room?

Add up wall areas (length × height), multiply by coats, divide by the can's coverage per gallon, and round up.

Should I buy extra paint for touch-ups?

Yes — one spare gallon (or at least a quart) stored properly lasts years and saves you from an impossible color match later.
