About this gravel calculator

Driveways, paths, and shed pads all start with the same volume math: length × width × depth. Quarries sell aggregate by the cubic yard or by weight — a cubic yard of crushed stone weighs roughly 1.4 tons (denser rock like trap rock runs heavier, lightweight aggregates lighter). This calculator reports both so you can compare quotes either way.

Depth is the planning decision. A pedestrian path works at 2–3 inches; a residential driveway wants 4–6 inches of compacted aggregate over a prepared subgrade. And "compacted" is doing work in that sentence — loose gravel settles 20–30%, so a 4-inch loose layer becomes about 3 inches of driveway. Order accordingly.

For driveways, the pro move is layers: larger crusher-run stone at the bottom for structure, smaller angular gravel on top for a tight surface. Round pea gravel looks nice in garden paths but migrates under tires — use angular crushed stone where vehicles drive.

How to use

  1. Measure the length and width of the area in feet.
  2. Enter the loose depth in inches — 4 inches is typical for driveways.
  3. Press Calculate for cubic yards and tons.
  4. Add ~10% for compaction; confirm tons-per-yard with your quarry.

Good to know

  • Tons-per-yard varies by rock type (1.3–1.6 common) — confirm with your supplier.
  • Does not include subgrade prep, geotextile fabric, or edging — all worth budgeting.
  • Irregular areas: measure the bounding rectangle and adjust down for curves.

Frequently asked questions

How much gravel do I need for a driveway?

A 50×12 ft driveway at 4 inches deep: 50 × 12 × (4/12) = 200 cu ft ≈ 7.4 cubic yards (≈10.4 tons). Add 10% for compaction.

How many tons of gravel are in a cubic yard?

Roughly 1.4 tons per cubic yard for crushed stone. Dense rock runs heavier; confirm with your quarry.

How deep should a gravel driveway be?

4–6 inches of compacted aggregate over a firm subgrade. Less than 4 inches ruts quickly under vehicles.

What is the difference between crushed stone and pea gravel?

Crushed stone is angular and locks together — best for driveways. Pea gravel is rounded and shifts under tires — best for paths and landscaping.

How do you calculate gravel?

Length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in ÷ 12) = cubic feet; divide by 27 for cubic yards; multiply yards by ~1.4 for tons.
