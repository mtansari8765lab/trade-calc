About this fence post concrete calculator

A fence post hole is a cylinder with a post standing in it, so the concrete you need is the hole volume minus the post volume: concrete per post = (π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × depth − post cross-section × depth) ÷ 1,728, in cubic feet. The post deduction matters — a 4×4 post displaces about 0.21 cu ft in a 30-inch hole, roughly 15% of the hole — and skipping it is why bag-count estimates from hole volume alone always overbuy. Bags are the total concrete divided by the bag yield: about 0.6 cu ft for an 80 lb bag, 0.45 for 60 lb, 0.3 for 40 lb.

Size the hole to the post and the job. A 10-inch diameter hole is the standard for a 4×4 post (about three times the post width); use 12 inches for 6×6 posts, gate posts, and windy or exposed runs. Depth is the structural number: at least one-third of the post height above ground, minimum 2 ft, and below the local frost line in cold climates — a post set above the frost line heaves. Dig or auger the hole, drop in 2–3 inches of gravel for drainage if the soil holds water, set the post, and pour.

Worked example: one 4×4 post in a 10-inch diameter, 30-inch deep hole. Hole volume is π × 5² × 30 ÷ 1,728 = 1.36 cu ft; the post (3½ × 3½ in actual) displaces 0.21 cu ft, so the concrete is 1.15 cu ft per post — 2 bags of 80 lb mix. For a run of 8 posts with a 10% waste allowance: 1.15 × 8 × 1.1 = 10.13 cu ft, 17 bags of 80 lb. Enter your own hole and post sizes above for your fence.

Premix and fast-setting mix take the same volume — the math does not change, only the setting. Fast-setting (Postcrete-style) mix is poured dry into the hole and watered in place, which suits one post at a time; standard premix is mixed in a barrow and suits a run of posts. Either way, brace the post plumb while it sets and do not hang rails or panels until the mix has cured — usually 24 to 48 hours, longer in cold or wet weather. How to use

  1. Enter the hole diameter and depth in inches — measure the auger or the dug hole, not the post.
  2. Pick the post size: 4×4 and 6×6 use the actual lumber dimensions (3½ and 5½ inches), which is what the post really displaces.
  3. Enter the number of posts and your bag size (or ready-mix for a large run), plus a waste allowance — 10% covers uneven holes.
  4. Press Calculate for concrete per post, total concrete, and the bags to buy.

Good to know

  • Assumes a round hole. A square dug hole holds more concrete than the same nominal size in this calculator — measure the widest point and treat it as the diameter.
  • Post deduction uses actual lumber dimensions (4×4 = 3½ × 3½ in). A rough-sawn or oversized post displaces slightly more.
  • Bag yields are approximate (≈ 0.6 / 0.45 / 0.3 cu ft) — check the yield printed on your bag; some mixes vary by 5–10%.
  • Does not include the gravel base under the post. Allow 2–3 inches of gravel per hole for drainage, on top of the hole depth.
  • Depth guidance is a rule of thumb. Frost depth, wind exposure, and gate loads vary by location — local code governs.

Formula & standards

Formula: Concrete per post = (π × (hole diameter ÷ 2)² × hole depth − post cross-section × hole depth) ÷ 1,728 cubic feet; bags = ceil(total concrete × (1 + waste) ÷ bag yield).

Standard: None — cylinder-minus-post geometry; bag yields (≈0.6 cu ft for 80 lb, 0.45 for 60 lb, 0.3 for 40 lb) are the standard premix figures printed on the bag. Hole depth should reach local frost depth per code.

Waste allowance: Uneven augered or dug holes and over-pour at the top; a 10% allowance covers both.

Frequently asked questions

How much concrete do I need for one fence post?

Hole volume minus post volume. A 4×4 post in a 10-inch diameter, 30-inch deep hole needs 1.15 cu ft of concrete — 2 bags of 80 lb mix. Enter your own hole and post sizes above; the calculator deducts the post for you.

How many bags of concrete per fence post?

For the standard 4×4 post in a 10 × 30 inch hole, 2 bags of 80 lb mix (or 3 bags of 60 lb). Larger holes, deeper holes, and 6×6 posts take more — a 6×6 in a 12-inch, 36-inch hole needs 3 bags of 80 lb per post.

How deep should a fence post hole be?

At least one-third of the post height above ground, with a 2 ft minimum, and below the local frost line in cold climates. A 6 ft fence wants a 2 ft hole as a minimum; in frost country the frost depth can push that to 3 ft or more.

What size hole do I dig for a 4x4 fence post?

10 inches in diameter is the standard — about three times the post width, leaving room to work the concrete around the post. Use 12 inches for 6×6 posts, gate posts, and exposed or windy runs.

Can I use fast-setting concrete for fence posts?

Yes. Fast-setting (Postcrete-style) mix uses the same volume as standard premix — pour it dry into the hole, add water, and brace the post plumb. It suits one post at a time; for a long run, standard premix mixed in a barrow is usually cheaper.

Should I subtract the post volume from the concrete?

Yes — the post displaces roughly 15% of a standard hole, and skipping the deduction overbuys by about a bag per 6 posts. This calculator deducts the post automatically, using its actual dimensions.
