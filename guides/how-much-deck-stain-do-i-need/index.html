How Much Deck Stain Do I Need? Gallons, Coats & Worked Examples

Planning estimate, not a staining quote. Deck stain quantity is area times coats divided by coverage — and coverage depends on your wood, not on a generic figure. This guide gives you the rates — then run your deck through our deck stain calculator.

The formula

Gallons = deck area × (1 + railing allowance) × coats ÷ coverage per gallon, rounded up. Coverage per gallon: about 300 sq ft on smooth planed wood, 250 on standard boards, 200 on rough-sawn or weathered lumber — because weathered wood drinks stain. Railings and spindles add roughly 20% on top of the floor area (some guides use 15%; railings with many balusters push toward 30%). A gallon of stain covers about 175 sq ft by some product figures — which is why the coverage printed on your can governs over any generic rate here.

Coats

Two coats is standard: the first coat soaks in and seals, the second builds the wear layer and the color. One coat suits a refresh on a recently stained deck in good condition; three coats (or a stain-plus-sealer system) suits heavy wear areas. A 12 × 10 ft deck (120 sq ft) needs about 2 gallons for two coats at 250 sq ft per gallon — and a 5-gallon bucket covers roughly 1,250 sq ft for one coat, which is enough for most standard decks in a single coat but not for two.

Prep and conditions

Stain only when the deck is dry and above 50°F, with no rain for 48 hours, and new pressure-treated wood needs 6–12 months to dry before it takes stain evenly. Clean first — power wash or scrub with deck cleaner, then let dry 48 hours — and sand lightly (80–100 grit) to knock down splinters. Stain in the shade: sun dries stain too fast and leaves lap marks. Work in full rows holding a wet edge, wait for the recoat window (usually 2–4 hours) between coats, and stay off the deck 24–48 hours after the last coat.

The batch rule

Buy stain in one batch: stain color varies between batches, and a deck finished from two batches can show a visible line in raking light. Keep the leftover can labeled for touch-ups — a labeled can saves you from an impossible color match later. Stain or paint? Stain soaks in and does not peel; paint sits on top and chips. Stain is easier to maintain and refinish, which is why most decks are stained. Semi-transparent stain lasts roughly 2–4 years, solid stain 3–5, depending on sun exposure.

Worked examples

  1. 12 × 14 deck, 1 coat, 250 sq ft/gal, no railings: 168 ÷ 250 = 0.67 → 1 gallon.
  2. Same deck, 2 coats, railings at 20%: 168 × 1.20 × 2 ÷ 250 = 1.61 → 2 gallons.
  3. 16 × 24 deck, 2 coats, 250 sq ft/gal, railings: 384 × 1.20 × 2 ÷ 250 = 3.69 → 4 gallons.
  4. 12 × 12 deck, 2 coats, 250 sq ft/gal, no railings: 144 × 2 ÷ 250 = 1.15 → 2 gallons.

Frequently asked questions

How many gallons of stain for a 12x12 deck?

A 12 × 12 deck is 144 sq ft; at 2 coats and 250 sq ft per gallon with no railings, that is 1.15 gallons — buy 2 gallons. With railings at 20%, it is 1.38 gallons — still 2 gallons.

How many square feet does a gallon of deck stain cover?

About 300 sq ft on smooth planed wood, 250 on standard boards, and 200 on rough or weathered lumber, per coat.

How many gallons of stain for a 12x14 deck?

168 sq ft needs about 1 gallon for one coat at 250 sq ft per gallon, and 2 gallons for two coats with railings.

Does a 5-gallon bucket cover a whole deck?

A 5-gallon bucket covers about 1,250 sq ft for one coat — enough for most standard decks in one coat, but two coats on a large deck need more.

Should I stain or paint my deck?

Stain soaks in and does not peel; paint sits on top and chips. Stain is easier to maintain and refinish, which is why most decks are stained.

The rest of the deck job

Stain finishes the deck — figure deck boards for the build and the cost to build the deck when budgeting the whole project.
