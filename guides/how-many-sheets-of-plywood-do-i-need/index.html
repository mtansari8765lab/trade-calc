How Many Sheets of Plywood Do I Need? Formula, Sizes & Worked Examples

Planning estimate — the waste allowance is the whole game; a part-sheet still has to be bought whole. A 4 × 8 ft sheet covers 32 sq ft. This guide gives you the formula, then run your area through our plywood calculator.

The formula

Sheets = ceiling(area × (1 + waste) ÷ sheet area). A 4 × 8 ft sheet is 32 sq ft, a 4 × 10 ft sheet is 40 sq ft, and a 4 × 12 ft sheet is 48 sq ft. A 20 × 12 ft subfloor is 240 sq ft; with 10% waste that is 264 sq ft ÷ 32 = 8.25, rounded up to 9 sheets of 4 × 8 ft. The round-up matters: 8.25 sheets cannot be bought, and the ninth sheet covers the cut pieces that do not divide evenly.

Sheet sizes and layout waste

The standard sheet is 4 × 8 ft (32 sq ft), with 4 × 10 ft and 4 × 12 ft also stocked for long runs with fewer joints. Waste of 10% suits a plain rectangle laid in straight rows, where the offcut from the end of one row usually starts the next. Use 15% for L-shaped rooms, floors with many openings, or a diagonal layout: every cut leaves an offcut too short or too narrow to reuse, and a 45-degree layout can waste 15% or more. Stagger the end joints — joints in adjacent rows should land at least 16 inches apart, one joist bay — and never line up four sheet corners at one point, which is the classic subfloor squeak.

Plywood or OSB?

Both are sheet goods and this calculator counts either — the sheet sizes and the math are identical. The difference is the job: OSB is the usual subfloor and roof sheathing (cheaper, and its strands interlock well), while plywood is the usual choice where the sheet will be seen, bent, or exposed to repeated wetting (it holds screws better at edges and dries out without swelling as much). Thickness is a separate decision that does not change the count: 3/4-in (23/32-in) for subfloors on 16-in joist spacing, 1/2-in for roof sheathing, 1/4-in for underlayment.

Measuring an irregular area

For an L-shaped room, split it into two rectangles, measure each, and add the areas — never estimate a room's area from its longest dimensions, which overcounts the missing corner. For a floor with a fireplace bump-out or a closet notch, measure the full rectangle, then subtract the notch as its own rectangle. For a roof, measure each slope separately (the slope length, not the horizontal run) and add them; a roof's slope area is always larger than its footprint.

Worked examples

  1. 20 × 12 ft subfloor, 4 × 8 ft sheets, 10% waste: 240 sq ft → 264 with waste → 9 sheets.
  2. 12 × 10 ft room, 4 × 8 ft sheets, 0% waste: 120 sq ft ÷ 32 = 3.75 → 4 sheets.
  3. 30 × 24 ft roof slope, 4 × 8 ft sheets, 15% waste: 720 sq ft → 828 with waste → 26 sheets.

Common mistakes

Three errors cover most sheet shortages: forgetting the waste allowance entirely (an exact count always runs short, because the last sheet in each row is cut), measuring the horizontal footprint of a roof instead of the slope length (the slope is longer, so the count comes up short), and lining up four corners at one point, which squeaks and is hard to fix after the floor is down. Buy the spare sheet if the supplier is far away — an unopened sheet returns, a half-covered floor does not.

Frequently asked questions

How many sheets of plywood do I need for a 20x12 floor?

240 sq ft with 10% waste is 264 sq ft ÷ 32 = 8.25, rounded up to 9 sheets of 4 × 8 ft plywood.

How many square feet does a sheet of plywood cover?

A 4 × 8 ft sheet covers 32 sq ft; a 4 × 10 ft sheet covers 40 sq ft; a 4 × 12 ft sheet covers 48 sq ft.

What waste percentage should I add for plywood?

10% for a plain rectangle in straight rows; 15% for L-shaped rooms, many openings, or a diagonal layout.

Is OSB the same as plywood for counting sheets?

Yes — the sheet sizes and the math are identical. They differ in use, not in the count.

How thick should subfloor plywood be?

3/4-in (23/32-in) is standard for subfloors on 16-in joist spacing. Thickness does not change the sheet count.
