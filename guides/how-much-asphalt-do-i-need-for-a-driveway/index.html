How Much Asphalt Do I Need for a Driveway? Formula, Thickness & Worked Examples

Planning estimate, not a paving quote. Asphalt is ordered by the ton, and the tonnage follows one formula plus one judgment call: the thickness. This guide gives you both — then run your driveway through our asphalt calculator.

The formula

Tons = length (ft) × width (ft) × thickness (ft) × 145 lb per cubic foot ÷ 2,000. The 145 lb/ft³ figure is the standard in-place density for compacted hot-mix asphalt (the Asphalt Institute range is 142–148). A shortcut: one ton covers about 83 sq ft at 2 inches, 55 sq ft at 3 inches, and 41 sq ft at 4 inches. Divide your area by the coverage figure for a quick check against the full formula.

Thickness: the judgment call

3 inches of compacted asphalt over a 4–8 inch crushed-stone base is the standard residential starting point. Two inches works only as an overlay on structurally sound existing pavement or for very light use; it is too thin for a new driveway and cracks sooner. Four inches or more suits heavy vehicles, delivery traffic, and freeze-thaw climates. What you enter is compacted depth — the loose layer the paver lays is thicker before rolling, so do not inflate the number for compaction; the waste allowance covers that.

Base prep matters more than the asphalt

Most asphalt failures are base failures, not asphalt failures. A proper job excavates to depth, compacts the subgrade, and lays 4–8 inches of crushed stone, compacted in lifts, before the asphalt goes down. Skipping base prep to save money buys a driveway that ruts and cracks within a few seasons. If your quote has no base work in it, it is not a comparable quote.

Hot mix vs cold mix

Hot mix is the right choice for 95% of projects: heated to 300–350°F, it provides superior durability and lasts 15–20 years with maintenance. Cold mix is only for temporary repairs and pothole patches — it lasts 1–3 years and is not a driveway material. Bagged cold-mix makes sense for a patch; a driveway needs a plant order.

Worked examples

  1. 50 × 10 ft driveway, 3 in: 500 sq ft × 0.25 ft = 125 cu ft × 145 ÷ 2,000 = 9.06 tons — order 9.52 tons with 5% waste.
  2. 40 × 20 ft driveway, 3 in: 800 sq ft × 0.25 ft = 200 cu ft = 14.5 tons before waste.
  3. 20 × 20 ft driveway, 3 in: 400 sq ft × 0.25 ft = 100 cu ft = 7.25 tons before waste.

Cost context

Material alone runs roughly $90–$200 per ton in 2026 depending on region (about $120 national average for hot mix), with the Southeast cheapest and the West Coast most expensive. Small residential orders cost more per ton than bulk plant prices because of minimum charges and delivery. A fully installed driveway typically budgets $3–$12 per square foot depending on region, base work, demolition, drainage, and access. Sealcoating every 3–5 years extends life noticeably.

Frequently asked questions

How much asphalt do I need for a 50x10 driveway?

A 50 × 10 ft driveway at 3 inches thick needs 9.06 tons before waste — order about 9.52 tons with a 5% allowance.

How many square feet does one ton of asphalt cover?

About 83 sq ft at 2 inches thick, 55 sq ft at 3 inches, and 41 sq ft at 4 inches, at 145 lb per cubic foot.

How thick should an asphalt driveway be?

At least 3 inches of compacted asphalt over a 4–8 inch crushed-stone base. Two inches suits overlays only; heavy vehicles and cold climates need 4 inches or more.

Hot mix vs cold mix asphalt — which should I use?

Hot mix for anything permanent: new driveways, parking areas, and roads. Cold mix is only for temporary patches and pothole repairs.

How long does an asphalt driveway last?

A well-built asphalt driveway often lasts 15–25 years. Drainage, base support, traffic, climate, construction quality, crack sealing, and timely repairs matter more than any single age estimate.
