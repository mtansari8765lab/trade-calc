How Much Grass Seed Do I Need? Formula, Rates & Worked Examples

Planning estimate — the rate is set by the grass, and the rate on your bag governs. Seed is sold by the pound at a rate per 1,000 sq ft. This guide gives you the formula, then run your lawn through our grass seed calculator.

The formula

Pounds = area ÷ 1,000 × rate (lb per 1,000 sq ft), with overseeding at about half the new-lawn rate. A 2,000 sq ft new lawn in tall fescue at 6 lb per 1,000 sq ft needs 12 lb of seed; overseeding the same lawn needs about 6 lb. Seed bags come in fixed sizes (commonly 3, 7, 10, 20, and 50 lb), so buy the bag size above your pounds, not below — a thin lawn is the expensive mistake, and leftover seed keeps for a season in a dry place.

Rates by grass type

The rate is set by seed size: Kentucky bluegrass seed is tiny, so about 3 lb per 1,000 sq ft covers it; tall fescue seed is large, about 6 lb; perennial ryegrass about 8 lb; fine fescue about 5 lb; bermuda (hulled) about 2 lb. Those are typical new-lawn rates. Blends differ, and coated seed is heavier: a bag that is 50% coating by weight needs twice the pounds to put down the same seed, so read the pure-live-seed percentage on the label, not just the bag weight — and use the rate printed on your bag over any guide.

New lawn vs overseeding

A new lawn is seeded at the full rate onto bare soil, because every square foot needs seed. Overseeding thickens an existing lawn and uses about half the new-lawn rate — the existing grass already covers most of the ground, and the new seed only needs to fill the thin spots. Overseed in early fall for cool-season grasses (fescue, bluegrass, ryegrass), when the soil is still warm and the weeds are dying back; overseed warm-season bermuda in late spring. Mow short and rake or aerate first so the seed reaches soil instead of sitting on thatch.

Prep that makes seed take

Seed fails most often from the surface, not the rate. Seed that dries out once after germinating dies, so the first two weeks need light watering two or three times a day, keeping the top inch damp without puddling. Seed sown on compacted soil washes away or never contacts the ground — rake the top inch loose first. Spread evenly by splitting the seed in half and walking the lawn in one direction with half, then at right angles with the rest, and press it in with a roller or your feet so every seed touches soil. Do not bury seed deep: most lawn seed wants to sit at or just under the surface.

Worked examples

  1. 2,000 sq ft new lawn, tall fescue: 2 × 6 = 12 lb.
  2. 2,000 sq ft overseed, tall fescue: 2 × 3 = 6 lb.
  3. 5,000 sq ft new lawn, perennial ryegrass: 5 × 8 = 40 lb (buy a 50 lb bag).

Seed or sod?

Seed is the cheaper, slower route — roughly a tenth of the cost of sod for the same area, with 2–3 weeks to germination and a full season to fill in. Sod is an instant lawn, laid in a day, but it costs far more and needs just as much watering in its first weeks. Seed suits a patient budget and a lawn that can wait a season; sod suits a deadline, a slope where seed would wash away, or a lawn that must be used immediately. Frequently asked questions

How much grass seed do I need for 2,000 square feet?

A 2,000 sq ft new lawn in tall fescue at 6 lb per 1,000 sq ft needs 12 lb of seed. Overseeding the same lawn needs about 6 lb.

How much grass seed per 1,000 square feet?

Tall fescue about 6 lb, perennial ryegrass about 8 lb, Kentucky bluegrass about 3 lb, fine fescue about 5 lb, bermuda about 2 lb per 1,000 sq ft for a new lawn.

How much grass seed do I need for overseeding?

About half the new-lawn rate — for example, about 3 lb per 1,000 sq ft for tall fescue instead of 6 lb.

Is grass seed cheaper than sod?

Yes — seed costs roughly a tenth of sod for the same area, but it takes 2–3 weeks to germinate and a full season to fill in, while sod is an instant lawn.

Why did my grass seed not grow?

The usual causes are seed drying out after germinating, seed sown on compacted soil with poor seed-to-soil contact, or seed buried too deep.
