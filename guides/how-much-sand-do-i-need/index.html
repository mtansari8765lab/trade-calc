How Much Sand Do I Need? Formula, Depths & Worked Examples

Planning estimate — the bag governs on small jobs, the yard on big ones. Sand is ordered by volume and paid for by weight, so this guide gives you both, then run your area through our sand calculator.

The formula

Cubic feet = length × width × depth in feet. Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27. Tons = cubic yards × 1.35 (dry sand, about 2,700 lb per cubic yard). A 10×10 ft area at 1 inch deep: 100 × (1 ÷ 12) = 8.33 cu ft = 0.31 cu yd = 0.42 tons. With 10% waste that is 0.34 cu yd, or 19 bags of 50 lb sand (each bag about 0.5 cu ft).

Depth by project

Paver bedding: spread about 1.2 inches loose so it compacts to 1 inch finished (ICPI guidance), and allow 15–25% extra for compaction. Sandbox: 6–12 inches of washed play sand. Lawn leveling: 1/4–1/2 inch per pass, in multiple light passes rather than one thick one. Pool base / leveling layer: typically 2–4 inches, compacted. The depth is the whole estimate — measure it, do not guess it, because doubling the depth doubles the order.

Which sand for which job

Concrete / sharp sand for paver bases and bedding — it locks together under compaction. Washed play sand for sandboxes — clean, silica-screened, and safe for children. Masonry sand for mortar mixes. Fill sand for bulk leveling where appearance does not matter. The weight per yard is similar across types, but the wrong type fails the job: play sand under pavers shifts, and sharp sand in a sandbox is not child-safe.

Bagged vs bulk

A 50 lb bag is about 0.5 cu ft, so a cubic yard is about 54 bags. Below about 20 bags, bagged is convenient and the delivery fee for bulk is not worth it. Above that, bulk by the cubic yard is almost always cheaper and far less hauling. A standard pickup truck safely carries about 1 cubic yard of sand — and wet sand weighs 20–30% more than dry, so check the payload before hauling a full yard of wet material.

Worked examples

  1. 10×10 ft paver base, 1 in: 8.33 cu ft = 0.31 cu yd = 0.42 tons; with 20% compaction allowance, 0.37 cu yd, or 20 bags of 50 lb.
  2. 4×4 ft sandbox, 8 in: 10.67 cu ft = 0.40 cu yd = 0.53 tons; about 22 bags of 50 lb play sand.
  3. 1,000 sq ft lawn, 1/2 in top-dressing: 41.67 cu ft = 1.54 cu yd = 2.08 tons; with 10% waste, 1.70 cu yd.

Common mistakes

Three errors cover most sand shortages: entering the depth in feet instead of inches (a 12× error), skipping the compaction allowance on paver bases, and ordering by tons for a small job where bags are the unit — 0.42 tons sounds small, but it is 19 bags to haul.

Frequently asked questions

How much sand do I need for a 10x10 area at 1 inch deep?

10 × 10 ft at 1 inch is 8.33 cu ft = 0.31 cubic yards = 0.42 tons. With 10% waste, order 0.34 cubic yards, or 19 bags of 50 lb sand.

How many bags of sand are in a cubic yard?

About 54 bags of 50 lb sand per cubic yard. For more than about 20 bags, bulk delivery by the cubic yard is usually cheaper.

How much sand do I need for a sandbox?

A 4×4 ft sandbox at 8 inches deep needs about 10.67 cu ft = 0.40 cubic yards, or about 22 bags of 50 lb washed play sand.

How deep should sand be under pavers?

Spread about 1.2 inches loose so it compacts to 1 inch finished, and allow 15–25% extra for compaction. Use concrete or sharp sand.

How much does a cubic yard of sand weigh?

About 1.35 tons (2,700 lb) dry. Wet sand weighs 20–30% more, so check your truck's payload before hauling a full yard.
