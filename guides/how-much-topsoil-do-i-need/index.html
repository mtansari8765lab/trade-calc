How Much Topsoil Do I Need? Formula, Depths & Worked Examples

Planning estimate, not a delivery quote. Every topsoil order is one formula plus one judgment call: the depth. This guide gives you both, with worked examples — then run your area through our topsoil calculator.

The formula

Cubic yards = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (ft) ÷ 27, because one cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. Depth is almost always measured in inches, so convert first (inches ÷ 12 = feet) — or use the shortcut: square feet × depth in inches ÷ 324. One cubic yard covers 324 square feet at 1 inch deep, or 81 square feet at 4 inches deep.

Depth targets by project

Depth is where orders go wrong, so match it to the job: leveling and thin cover 2–3 inches; new lawn 4–6 inches firmed; vegetable and flower beds 6–12 inches; raised beds filled to the box depth, often 12+ inches; topdressing an existing lawn just 1/2 inch per pass (more smothers the grass). When in doubt, measure the depth you actually want in place — not the depth of the loose pile.

Bags vs bulk

One cubic yard equals 27 one-cubic-foot bags, 14 two-cubic-foot bags, or about 36 forty-pound (0.75 cu ft) bags. Bagged soil costs several times more per yard than bulk, but wins for small patches where a delivery fee would dominate. The break-even sits around 1 cubic yard: below it, bags; above it, bulk delivery, even with a $50–$150 delivery fee. Bulk topsoil typically runs $15–$50 per yard depending on type and market.

Settling: order more than the math

Loose topsoil compacts as it is graded, watered, and walked on — the in-place depth ends up less than the loose depth you ordered. A 10% settling allowance is standard, 15% on uneven sub-grade. Round up to whole yards from a bulk supplier; the extra half-yard is cheaper than a second delivery.

Worked examples

  1. 10×10 bed at 6 inches: 100 sq ft × 6 in ÷ 324 = 1.85 cubic yards — order 2 yards.
  2. 1,000 sq ft lawn at 4 inches: 1,000 × 4 ÷ 324 = 12.35 cubic yards — about 14 with a settling allowance.
  3. 4×8 raised bed, 12 inches deep: 32 cu ft ÷ 27 = 1.19 cubic yards — order 1.5.
  4. Topdressing 1,000 sq ft at 1/2 inch: 1,000 × 0.5 ÷ 324 = 1.54 cubic yards.

Weight and hauling

Moist screened topsoil weighs roughly 1.35 tons per cubic yard — a 2-yard order is about 2.7 tons, which exceeds many half-ton pickups' payload, so check before you haul it yourself. Delivery trucks carry far more: a six-wheeler about 8 yards, a tri-axle about 20. Wetter or compost-heavy soil weighs more than the planning factor.

Frequently asked questions

How much topsoil do I need for a 10x10 bed at 6 inches?

10 × 10 ft at 6 inches deep is 50 cubic feet = 1.85 cubic yards. With a 10% settling allowance, order about 2.04 cubic yards — round up to 2 yards from a bulk supplier.

How many bags of topsoil are in a cubic yard?

27 one-cubic-foot bags, 14 two-cubic-foot bags, or about 36 forty-pound (0.75 cu ft) bags per cubic yard.

How deep should topsoil be for a new lawn?

4 to 6 inches firmed for a new lawn. Thin cover and leveling need only 2–3 inches; vegetable beds want 6–12 inches.

How much does a cubic yard of topsoil weigh?

Roughly 1.35 tons for moist screened topsoil. A 2-yard order is about 2.7 tons — check your vehicle's payload before hauling.

Is bulk or bagged topsoil cheaper?

Bulk, by a wide margin, once you need more than about 1 cubic yard. Bagged soil costs several times more per yard but wins for small patches where delivery fees dominate.

The rest of the landscaping order

Topsoil usually arrives with other materials — figure mulch for the beds, gravel for paths and drainage, and sod for the new lawn on top of it.
