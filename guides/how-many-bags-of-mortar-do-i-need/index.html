How Many Bags of Mortar Do I Need? Formula, Bag Yields & Worked Examples

Planning estimate — the bag rate is a best case, and the bag you buy governs. One 80 lb bag of premixed mortar lays up to 37 standard bricks or 13 standard blocks. This guide gives you the formula, then run your wall through our mortar calculator.

The formula

Units = wall area × units per sq ft (6.86 modular bricks or 1.125 standard 8×8×16 in blocks per sq ft at 3/8-in joints). Bags = ceiling(units ÷ bags per bag × (1 + waste)), where one 80 lb bag lays up to 37 bricks or 13 blocks. A 100 sq ft single-wythe modular brick wall: 100 × 6.86 = 686 bricks; 686 ÷ 37 = 18.5, so 19 bags at best, 21 bags with 10% waste. The same wall in block: 100 × 1.125 = 113 blocks; 113 ÷ 13 = 8.7, so 9 bags at best, 10 bags with 10% waste.

What one 80 lb bag lays

The figures come from the Quikrete Mortar Mix No. 1102 data sheet (Type N): one 80 lb bag lays up to 37 standard bricks or 13 standard 8×8×16 in blocks at 3/8-in joints. Sakrete lists 35 bricks or 12 blocks for its Type S mix — within 5–10% of Quikrete, so the calculator's rates are a fair planning figure for any major brand. A 60 lb bag lays roughly three-quarters as much (about 28 bricks or 10 blocks). Note the words "up to": the rated yield assumes a skilled mason, full beds, and 3/8-in joints. Mortar dropped on the ground, furrowed beds, and a tub left half-full all eat into it — budget one bag per 12 blocks or about 3 bags per 100 bricks once waste is counted, not the bare rated figure.

Mortar types (ASTM C270)

ASTM C270 defines four mortar types by strength, and the type is chosen by the wall, not the bag count — the yield is the same across types. Type N is the general-purpose mortar for most above-grade walls. Type S is stronger and is used below grade, for retaining walls, and where wind or soil loads are higher. Type M is the strongest, used for foundations and heavy loads. Type O is the weakest, for interior non-load-bearing work and repointing old soft brick, where a strong mortar would crack the brick. Premixed bags are sold by type (Quikrete Mason Mix is Type S, Mortar Mix is Type N) — buy the type your wall needs, then count bags.

Joints and bedding

Joint thickness moves the count more than most people expect: a 1/2-in joint uses roughly a third more mortar than the standard 3/8-in joint, because every bed and head joint is thicker. Block bedding matters too: face-shell bedding (mortar only on the two face shells of the block) is the standard and uses the rated yield, while fully bedded block (mortar across the whole block face, used where the wall must be grouted or is heavily loaded) uses roughly a third more. If your joints will be 1/2 in or your block fully bedded, use the 15% waste allowance in the calculator or step up a bag — the rated yield does not cover either case.

Worked examples

  1. 100 sq ft modular brick wall, 10% waste: 686 bricks, 686 ÷ 37 = 18.5 → 21 bags of 80 lb.
  2. 100 sq ft block wall, 10% waste: 113 blocks, 113 ÷ 13 = 8.7 → 10 bags of 80 lb.
  3. 160 sq ft block wall (20 ft × 8 ft), 10% waste: 180 blocks, 180 ÷ 13 = 13.8 → 16 bags of 80 lb.

When to stop buying bags

Past about 300 blocks or 1,000 bricks, site-mixed mortar (masonry cement + sand) is usually about half the cost of premixed bags. The field rule is about 30 blocks per bag of masonry cement mixed with roughly 240 lb of mason sand, or about 125 bricks per bag. For a small wall, bags are simpler and the waste is lower; for a house, a mixer and bulk sand pay for themselves quickly. Either way, count from the unit count first — the bag count follows it.

Frequently asked questions

How many bags of mortar do I need for 100 square feet of brick?

About 686 modular bricks, which is 19 bags of 80 lb mortar at best and 21 bags with 10% waste.

How many blocks does one 80 lb bag of mortar lay?

Up to 13 standard 8×8×16 in blocks at 3/8-in joints (Quikrete No. 1102 data sheet). Budget one bag per 12 blocks with waste.

How many bricks does one 80 lb bag of mortar lay?

Up to 37 standard bricks at 3/8-in joints. Budget about 3 bags per 100 bricks with waste.

What type of mortar should I use?

Type N for most above-grade walls, Type S below grade and for retaining walls, Type M for foundations, Type O only for interior non-load-bearing work and repointing (ASTM C270).

Does a wider joint use more mortar?

Yes — a 1/2-in joint uses roughly a third more mortar than a 3/8-in joint, and fully bedded block uses roughly a third more than face-shell bedding.
